Margins, unit costs and build costs: not in evidence.

2. **Ranking:** A > B > C.

3. **Strongest evidence for A:** Customers shown the choose-cadence offer bought again in 40/49 cases (81.6%). Customers shown no offer bought again in 34/84 cases (40.5%). The gap is about 39 points and holds within channel × packaging strata. This is an **association, not causal**: how offers were assigned is not documented, and seeing an offer is not the same as subscribing. A has no incentive in its design, so it has no discount leakage. It also matches the pause and cadence-change rule in Doc D.

4. **Objections:**
   - **B:** It reaches only customers with a recorded packaging issue (52/180). Its effect on reorders and the cost of replacements are not in evidence. Doc B says a leak damages trust beyond the value of the lost product, so a credit may not win the customer back.
   - **C:** An optional incentive leaks margin to well-timed customers who already reorder (31/52 = 59.6%). The timing gap of about 9–10 points is an association only. Customer volume, needed to check spend against INR 11 lakh, is not in evidence.

5. **What would change my ranking:** I would put B first if a randomized holdout shows A's lift disappears, or shows that people only sign up for A when given a discount.
