2. **Ranking (Operations):** A > C > B.

3. **Strongest evidence for A:** 40/49 customers offered "Choose cadence" (81.6%) bought again, against 34/84 not offered a plan (40.5%). The gap of about 39 points holds within each channel and packaging-issue group, so packaging does not explain it. This is an association, not proof of cause: we don't know how offers were assigned, and being shown an offer is not the same as subscribing. A needs no packaging redesign and already meets the pause and cadence-change rule (Doc D).

4. **Objections:**
   - **C:** It puts the least load on operations, but the evidence is weak. Customers whose reorder date was within 7 days of their consumption estimate bought again at 31/52 (59.6%), against about 50% for those outside that window. Timing by segment is rough when consumption ranges from 12 to 83 days. A subscription version would still need pause and cadence change (Doc D).
   - **B:** Replacements ship in the same packaging (52/180 orders had a recorded issue) and add checking work. It makes up for issues without lowering the issue rate, since redesign is out of scope (Doc D). Recovery has not been tested.

5. **What would change my ranking:** I would put C first if a trial run of A shows either of these:
   - pause or cadence changes can't run in today's ordering and delivery process
   - plan shipments have more packaging issues than today's 52/180

   There is no evidence on failed-delivery rates; the data only shows days of delay.
