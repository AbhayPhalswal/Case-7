1. **Claim:** "60-day outcome window" and a cadence "pre-selected to the option nearest their estimated consumption days" | **Evidence:** 12 of the 76 eligible customers (estimated 53–60 days) would default to the 60-day cadence. Their first refill would come on or after day 60, yet 10 of the 12 already buy again within 60 days today. | **Why:** The main KPI is built to count against the plan, so a good plan could get a "revise" or "stop". | **Change:** Make the main KPI a refill within 90 days of first delivery, and report results by default cadence.

2. **Claim:** "about +39 pts within channel x issue strata" | **Evidence:** This is observed data, not a test (Doc A; data dictionary). We don't know who was shown the cadence offer or on what terms. The cadence counts inside each channel × issue group are only 4–16, and Social commerce is 13/13. Only one option is offered, but Doc E asks for alternatives. | **Why:** It can't show whether timed reminders or the plan itself drives the gain. | **Change:** Run three arms: control, reminder timed to consumption only, and cadence plan. Call +39 an association, not an effect.

3. **Claim:** "Customers with a recorded packaging issue (52/180) do not get the plan offer" | **Evidence:** Issue customers shown the choose-cadence offer bought again at 11/14, against 8/38 under Fixed 30-day or Not offered. That is the largest gap in the data. The recovery has no comparison group. | **Why:** This leaves out 29% of customers, and the recovery spend teaches us nothing. | **Change:** After a verified recovery, randomise these customers to recovery only or recovery plus the plan offer.

4. **Claim:** "assume 800 cases x INR 65 = INR 52,000" | **Evidence:** 29% of 2,000 is about 580, not 800. If the 2,000 are the eligible 76/180 share of first orders, there would be about 1,370 issue cases, or about INR 0.89 lakh. The 2,000 customers and INR 3 lakh have no source. Replacement cost is not priced and could push the average over INR 65. There is no break-even logic (Doc C). | **Why:** Total spend is still within INR 11 lakh (about 3.9 lakh), but the 10-point scale rule has no margin basis. | **Change:** Give a source for volumes. State break-even uplift = spend ÷ (treated customers × contribution per refill). Scale only if the lower end of the confidence interval (about ±4.4 points at 1,000 per arm) clears it.

5. **Claim:** "Channels: email, WhatsApp and website account" | **Evidence:** 34 of the 76 eligible are Marketplace customers. Doc B3 says they may not recognise a direct subscription offer. | **Why:** If the offer can't reach them, the measured effect is watered down. | **Change:** Check how many can be contacted before launch, randomise within each channel, add an in-pack QR code for Marketplace, and report Marketplace separately.

6. **Claim:** "packaging issues on plan shipments — owner Operations Head" | **Evidence:** 52/180 first orders had an issue. Doc B2 says leaks damage trust, and Doc D rules out packaging redesign. | **Why:** Automatic shipments at about a 29% issue rate can undo the repeat buying they create, and no trigger is set. | **Change:** Add guardrails on the plan-shipment issue rate and on pauses and cancellations, recover automatically on each issue, and stop if the issue rate is above control.
