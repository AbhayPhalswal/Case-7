Executive summary & Q6 · Final board recommendation Q6 · 15 marks

Decision: run a 3-arm randomized pilot that tests a flexible refill plan (customer-chosen cadence defaulted to estimated consumption; pause, skip, change any time) against a consumption-timed reminder only and today's experience — with no discount. Customers with a recorded packaging issue get verified-issue recovery first. Scale only if a randomized lift clears break-even without guardrail breaches.
Targetsubscription_offer ∈ {Not offered, Fixed 30-day} AND packaging_issue = "None" AND first_order_returned = 0 AND estimated_consumption_days ≤ 60 → 76/180 (42.2%, CI 35.2–49.5); 48 Not offered + 28 Fixed; 34 marketplace, 26 website, 16 social. Exclusions (waterfall 180→131→93→89→76): already offered choose-cadence (49), packaging issue (38 → recovery cell), returned (4 → service follow-up), consumption >60 d (13 → later longer-cadence test).
InterventionArm P – Plan: after first delivery, opt-in refill plan; default cadence = estimated consumption days; reminder 5 days before each dispatch with one-click skip/pause/change (Doc D). Arm R – Reminder: one reorder reminder 5 days before estimated run-out, no subscription. Control: current experience. Channels: email, WhatsApp, website account; marketplace buyers only where consented contact exists, message names FreshNest as the brand they bought (Doc B3). Recovery cell: issue customers → verify → replacement or credit (avg ≤ ₹65) → randomized 1:1 recovery-only vs recovery + plan offer. Enrol until 470 per arm (no volume data in evidence, so duration is set by sample, not weeks) + 60-day follow-up.
Budget logicScreening: 3 × 470 = 1,410 eligible ÷ (76/180) = 3,339 first-order customers screened. Incentives: Arms P and R = ₹0. Recovery: 3,339 × 52/180 = 965 issue cases × 50–100% verified [assumption] × ₹65 = ₹0.31–0.63 L. Fixed (pause/cadence set-up, messaging, analytics): not in evidence → ring-fenced ₹3–6 L [proposal]; Ops quote ≤ ₹6 L is a launch gate. Total ₹3.3–6.6 L ≤ ₹11 L; ≥ ₹4.4 L unspent reserve. Break-even: required lift (pts) = 100 × variable cost per treated customer ÷ contribution margin per order (Finance to supply; not in evidence).
ExperimentIndividual randomization 1:1:1, stratified by channel × product family. 470/arm detects a 10-pt lift (52.6% → 62.6%) at 80% power, two-sided α = 0.025 per comparison (2 comparisons vs control); CI half-width ≈ ±6.4 pts. Decision window: 60 days after the last enrolment (90-day readout pre-registered).
KPIsPrimary: kept second order ≤ 60 days of first delivery (voluntary, or scheduled and not cancelled/returned within 7 days). Leading: plan opt-in rate; reminder click-through; share of plans with cadence within ±7 d of consumption. Guardrails: packaging-issue rate on plan shipments ≤ control; returns/cancellations ≤ control; contribution margin per customer ≥ control.
Decision rules [proposal]Scale P (or R if R is within 3 pts of P — cheaper, no subscription build) if lift ≥ 10 pts, 95% CI lower bound > break-even, all guardrails hold. Revise if lift 0–10 pts, or 60-day null but 90-day positive, or opt-in < 15% → change default cadence/message, re-test 4 weeks. Stop if lift ≤ 0, or plan-shipment issue rate > control, or margin per customer < control.
Risks · owners① The 41-pt gap is selection, not effect (offer assignment undocumented) → decide only on randomized control; no forecast from 81.6% — Growth Director. ② Plan shipments repeat packaging failures (52/180 had issues; Doc B2) → auto-recovery on every issue; issue-rate stop rule — Operations Head. ③ Margin leakage → no discount in P/R; credit only for verified issues, avg ≤ ₹65 — Finance Head.

Trade-off: we give up a fast discount-led push for a slower, margin-safe test that tells us whether the plan or simply better timing works. Remaining uncertainty: the causal size of the cadence effect and margin per order. Next step (week 0): Ops confirms pause/cadence capability and quote; Finance supplies margin per order; Growth confirms consented contact lists.

Two material dataset limitations

  • Not randomized, small, synthetic. Offer assignment is undocumented, so Finding 1 may reflect who was shown the offer; choose-cadence cells by channel × issue hold only 4–16 customers (social commerce 13/13). Observables are balanced (mean discount 20.2/20.2/21.4%, satisfaction 7.57/7.62/7.59) but unobservables are not.
  • Margin-blind. No order value, contribution margin, unit costs, offer acceptance or weekly volume — margin impact and break-even cannot be computed from the data.

Causality boundary — claims the evidence does not justify

"Bigger first-order discounts create loyalty": 20% → 36/58 = 62.1% but 30% → 24/51 = 47.1% and 40% → 10/16* = 62.5%; no steady pattern and not significant after adjustment (z = 1.3). Deal-seekers may self-select (case tension). Equally, "showing choose-cadence will raise repurchase by 41 pts" is an association, not an effect.

Data-definition issues that could change the decision

  • Offer shown ≠ subscribed. If scheduled deliveries count as a second purchase, part of 81.6% is mechanical → KPI counts only kept orders.
  • 60-day window vs consumption. 30/180 need >60 d to finish a pack (13/30 = 43.3%); "no purchase" may mean "not needed yet" → excluded from the 60-day target.
  • packaging_issue holds the text "None" (128 rows); default pandas reads it as missing — a tooling trap we checked for.

WeaknessEvidence check (Python recompute)Why it matters
